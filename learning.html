The Learning Curve

Doreen and I had been sailing for many years, our first efforts being in 1969/70 whilst service in the Royal Navy based in Malta. It was here that Alan passed this dinghy skippers ticket. Throughout the years since we had sailed in many different types of yacht. We considered ourselves knowledgable and good sailors, we really thought we knew everything there was to know about sailing. HOW WRONG WE WERE.


In the year 2000 we purchased our third yacht and the one we had wanted to be able to liveaboard and do some serious Ocean going sailing. We were very lucky to be able to purchase a new Island Packet 380 at the Southampton Boat Show in September 2000. These pages explain about the fitting out we did on Kiah, the things that worked well and the bits that failed to live up to expectations. We learnt as we went along, with input from more experienced sailors.

Please note that this is a list of the good and bad things that we did on Kiah. These are OUR experiences and we give our honest views, however, it is entirely possible that other yachtsmen have had different experiences with the items we list below, so each yachtsman and yachtswoman must make up their own mind as to the relevance of our comments made in these Learning Curve pages. We rate each item we used as GREEN for GOOD and met or exceeded our expectations, AMBER for SATISFACTORY and RED where an item FAILED in our view to provide the expected standard and therefore we replaced it.

Experiences & Hard Lessons Learned

ANCHORING

When we started out our Island Packet yacht was sold to us complete with a CQR anchor and 8mm chain. This ground tackle configuaration had worked reasonably well in the UK but we found it lacking when anchoring at sea. After much research and advice from Max, we decided to change to a SPADE anchor with 10mm chain. Our own example of the Pay Forward ethic was when Max gave me 50mtrs of 10mm chain and in return I troubleshooted his Computers. The new Spade and heavier chain worked much better in the various sea beds we encountered in the Mediterranean and Caribbean.

However,later on, experience taught us that when anchoring in bays open to various wind directions, it was much better to be able to pull our bows round into the wind (to ease side on preference of the yacht, which in turn led to uncomfortable days and nights). We did our research and found that a good kedge anchor would be the answer. It had to be a quality product, built to do the job but lightweight (as much as possible). Fortress FX anchors fitted the requirements exactly and so we purchased an FX37 and it proved a first class anchor and easy to use. We rate the Spade anchor and the Fortress as excellent purchases and therefore give them both a GREEN rating.
